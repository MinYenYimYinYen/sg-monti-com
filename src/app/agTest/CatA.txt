Category A – Chapter 1
Pest Management Terminology
1.	Pest: An undesirable organism that injures humans, plants, animals, products, or natural substances.
2.	Biological Control: Using natural enemies (predators, parasites, pathogens, competitors) to control pests.
3.	Chemical Control: Using naturally derived or synthetic chemicals (pesticides) to manage pests.
4.	Cultural Control: Practices that reduce pest establishment, reproduction, dispersal, and survival.
5.	Genetic Control: Breeding or selecting plants and animals to resist specific pest problems.
6.	Mechanical/Physical Control: Killing pests directly or making their environment unsuitable.
7.	Regulatory Control: Government-directed programs to prevent pest introduction and spread.
8.	Integrated Pest Management (IPM): A balanced, tactical approach to pest control using multiple methods to prevent pests from reaching damaging levels.
Pest Population Terminology
1.	Economic Threshold (ET): Pest population density at which control measures are needed to prevent reaching the economic injury level.
2.	Economic Injury Level (EIL): Pest population density that causes losses equal to the cost of control measures.
3.	Action Threshold: Pest level at which pest management action must be taken.
4.	Key Pests: Pests that cause major damage regularly unless controlled.
5.	Secondary Pests: Pests that become problematic when key pests are controlled.
6.	Occasional Pests: Pests that become troublesome only occasionally.
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Define "pest" Answer: A pest is an undesirable organism that causes harm to humans, plants, animals, products, or natural substances.
2.	State the four main groups of pests and give an example of each Answer:
•	Weeds (e.g., Canada thistle)
•	Invertebrates (e.g., brownbanded cockroach)
•	Pathogens (e.g., Hawthorne rust)
•	Vertebrates (e.g., rock dove)
3.	Discuss the importance of pest identification in pest control Answer: Accurate pest identification is crucial because it helps understand the pest's life cycle, behavior, and susceptible stages, which is essential for effective control strategies.
4.	List six general pest management methods Answer:
•	Biological Control
•	Chemical Control
•	Cultural Control
•	Genetic Control
•	Mechanical/Physical Control
•	Regulatory Control
5.	Define "Integrated Pest Management (IPM)" Answer: IPM is a balanced, tactical approach to pest control that uses multiple methods to prevent pests from reaching damaging levels with minimal environmental risk.
6.	List five benefits of using IPM Answer:
•	Preserves ecosystem balance
•	Addresses pesticide ineffectiveness
•	Saves money
•	Promotes a healthy environment
•	Maintains a good public image
7.	Discuss how using one or more control options can improve pest control Answer: Combining multiple pest control methods creates a more comprehensive and effective strategy, reducing reliance on a single approach and minimizing potential resistance or environmental harm.
8.	Explain how pest population levels trigger control procedures Answer: Pest population levels are monitored against economic thresholds and action thresholds. When pest populations reach these predetermined levels, specific control measures are implemented.

Chapter 2 – Regulations
Regulatory Terminology
1.	FIFRA (Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act): The primary federal law regulating pesticide production, transportation, sale, use, and disposal.
2.	EPA (Environmental Protection Agency): The federal agency responsible for administering FIFRA and regulating pesticide registration.
3.	Restricted-Use Pesticides (RUPs): Pesticides that can only be purchased and used by certified applicators due to higher toxicity or environmental risks.
4.	Tolerance: The maximum legal residue limit of a pesticide that may remain on food or feed products.
5.	Registration: The EPA process of evaluating and approving a pesticide for sale and use in the United States.
Applicator Classifications
1.	Private Applicator: A certified applicator who uses restricted-use pesticides for agricultural commodity production on their own or rented property.
2.	Commercial Applicator: A certified applicator who uses restricted-use pesticides for purposes other than private agricultural production.
Legal Terminology
1.	Labeling: The pesticide product's official instructions and information, which has the force of law.
2.	Certification: The process of recognizing an individual as competent to use or supervise the use of restricted-use pesticides.
3.	Reregistration: The EPA's process of reviewing older pesticides to ensure they meet current safety standards.
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Explain the purpose of federal pesticide regulations Answer: To protect public health, welfare, and the environment by managing the production, distribution, use, and disposal of pesticides while balancing their socioeconomic benefits.
2.	Describe the role of FIFRA in pesticide regulation Answer: FIFRA provides the framework for federal pesticide regulation, giving EPA the authority to register pesticides, set safety standards, and enforce compliance.
3.	Distinguish between restricted-use and general-use pesticides Answer: Restricted-use pesticides have higher toxicity or environmental risks and can only be purchased and applied by certified applicators, while general-use pesticides have lower risks and are available to the public.
4.	Explain the pesticide registration process Answer: Pesticide manufacturers submit scientific data to EPA, which reviews the potential risks to human health and the environment. If approved, the pesticide receives registration and can be sold and used.
5.	Define the concept of pesticide tolerance Answer: A tolerance is the maximum legal residue limit of a pesticide that may remain on food or feed products, ensuring food safety and protecting human health.
6.	Describe the types of certified applicators Answer:
•	Private Applicators: Use RUPs for agricultural commodity production on their own or rented property
•	Commercial Applicators: Use RUPs for various purposes not covered by private applicator definition
7.	List the key federal laws related to pesticide regulation Answer:
•	Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (FIFRA)
•	Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FFDCA)
•	Food Quality Protection Act (FQPA)
•	Endangered Species Act (ESA)
8.	Explain the importance of pesticide labeling Answer: Pesticide labels provide critical information about proper use, safety precautions, and legal restrictions. They have the force of law, and using a pesticide inconsistently with its label is a federal violation.
9.	Discuss the purpose of the Food Quality Protection Act (FQPA) Answer: FQPA set a higher safety standard for pesticides used on food, requiring EPA to consider aggregate exposure risks, focus on risks to infants and children, and review existing pesticide tolerances.
10.	Describe the recordkeeping requirements for pesticide applicators Answer: Applicators must maintain records of pesticide applications, which help document treatments, plan future purchases, provide medical information, and demonstrate environmental protection efforts.
Chapter 3 – Labels 
Label Terminology
1.	Pesticide Label: A legally binding document that provides comprehensive instructions for safe and effective pesticide use.
2.	Active Ingredient: The specific chemical responsible for the pesticide's pesticidal activity.
3.	Inert Ingredients: Substances in a pesticide formulation that are not directly responsible for pest control.
4.	Signal Words: Words indicating the relative acute toxicity of a pesticide (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION).
5.	Routes of Entry: Pathways through which a pesticide can enter the human body (oral, dermal, inhalation).
Regulatory Terminology
1.	EPA Registration Number: A unique identifier showing EPA approval of a pesticide product.
2.	Mandatory Statements: Label instructions that must be followed legally.
3.	Advisory Statements: Recommendations that provide additional guidance but are not legally binding.
4.	Safety Data Sheet (SDS): A detailed document providing comprehensive information about a chemical product.
Classification Terminology
1.	Restricted-Use Pesticide: A pesticide that can only be purchased and used by certified applicators.
2.	General-Use Pesticide: A pesticide available for purchase and use by the general public.
3.	Special Local Need (SLN) Registration: A state-specific pesticide registration allowing expanded or limited use.
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Distinguish between various types of pesticide registrations Answer:
•	Section 3: Standard registration for most pesticides
•	Section 24(c): Special local need registrations
•	Section 18: Emergency exemptions
•	Section 25(b): Minimum-risk pesticides
2.	Explain when to read the pesticide label Answer: Read the label before buying, before mixing and applying, when storing, and before disposing of the pesticide to ensure safe and legal use.
3.	State who may use a specific pesticide Answer: Depends on the classification:
•	Restricted-use pesticides: Only certified applicators
•	General-use pesticides: Anyone can purchase and use
4.	Identify the common, chemical, and brand/trade names of a pesticide Answer:
•	Chemical Name: Complex scientific name identifying chemical structure
•	Common Name: Shortened, officially accepted name
•	Trade/Brand Name: Manufacturer's registered product name
5.	Determine the percentage of active ingredient(s) in a formulation Answer: Check the ingredient statement on the label, which lists active ingredients and their percentages.
6.	Interpret the meaning of label signal words, symbols, and their relative hazard levels Answer:
•	DANGER-POISON: Highly toxic, potential for death
•	DANGER: Highly toxic by at least one route
•	WARNING: Moderately toxic
•	CAUTION: Slightly toxic
7.	Identify types of statements on a pesticide label Answer:
•	Precautionary
•	First aid
•	Personal protective equipment
•	Environmental hazards
•	Physical or chemical hazards
•	Mixing, loading, storage, and disposal instructions
8.	Describe how to interpret other documents and online resources referenced on the label Answer:
•	Check EPA-approved websites
•	Obtain referenced materials from manufacturers
•	Use Safety Data Sheets (SDS) in conjunction with the label
9.	Distinguish between advisory and mandatory statements on a label Answer:
•	Mandatory Statements: Direct, imperative instructions that must be followed
•	Advisory Statements: Recommendations that provide additional guidance
10.	Discuss how to use information on a Safety Data Sheet Answer: Use SDS to:
•	Understand detailed chemical properties
•	Get comprehensive safety information
•	Obtain emergency response procedures
•	Complement (not replace) the pesticide label

Chapter 4 – Formulations
Key Terms to Know 📚
Formulation Terminology
1.	Pesticide Formulation: A carefully crafted mixture of active and inert ingredients designed to create a usable end-product.
2.	Active Ingredient: The component responsible for controlling the target pest.
3.	Inert Ingredient: Components that improve the product's properties but have no pesticidal action.
4.	Technical Grade: The pure or concentrated form of the active ingredient before formulation.
5.	End-Use Product: The final pesticide product ready for application.
Formulation Types
1.	Solution (S): A homogeneous mixture where a substance is dissolved in a liquid.
2.	Suspension: A mixture of fine, solid particles dispersed in a liquid.
3.	Emulsion: A mixture of two liquids, where one is dispersed as droplets within the other.
4.	Concentrate: A formulation requiring mixing or dilution before use.
5.	Ready-to-Use (RTU): Formulations that can be applied directly without further dilution.
Abbreviation Terminology
1.	EC/E: Emulsifiable Concentrate
2.	WP/W: Wettable Powder
3.	G: Granules
4.	D: Dust
5.	ULV: Ultra-Low Volume
6.	RTU: Ready-to-Use
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Define a pesticide formulation and explain why formulations are necessary Answer: A pesticide formulation is a mixture of active and inert ingredients that makes the technical-grade active ingredient safer, easier to handle, and more effective by addressing issues like toxicity, handling difficulties, and application challenges.
2.	Describe the key components of a pesticide formulation Answer:
•	Carrier/Diluent: Dissolves or dilutes the active ingredient
•	Surface-Active Ingredients: Improve adhesion and spread
•	Additives: Enhance safety or pesticidal activity (stabilizers, dyes)
3.	Explain the differences between solutions, suspensions, and emulsions Answer:
•	Solution: Homogeneous, transparent, no settling
•	Suspension: Cloudy, requires agitation, particles do not dissolve
•	Emulsion: Milky appearance, droplets of one liquid dispersed in another
4.	Compare concentrates and ready-to-use formulations Answer:
•	Concentrates: Less expensive, require mixing, higher exposure risk
•	Ready-to-Use: More convenient, safer to handle, more expensive per unit area
5.	Interpret common formulation abbreviations on pesticide labels Answer: Understand abbreviations like:
•	EC: Emulsifiable Concentrate
•	WP: Wettable Powder
•	G: Granules
•	ULV: Ultra-Low Volume
•	RTU: Ready-to-Use
6.	List the factors to consider when choosing a pesticide formulation Answer:
•	Legal and labeled uses
•	Signal word (toxicity)
•	Applicator safety
•	Environmental safety
•	Pest biology
•	Site characteristics
•	Target surface
•	Application equipment
•	Application conditions
•	Potential surface damage
•	Cost
7.	Describe the advantages and disadvantages of liquid formulations Answer:
•	Emulsifiable Concentrates (EC): 
•	Advantages: Easy to handle, little agitation
•	Disadvantages: High concentration risk, skin absorption
•	Solutions: 
•	Advantages: Easy to measure, no agitation needed
•	Disadvantages: Limited availability, potential skin absorption
8.	Explain the characteristics of dry formulations Answer:
•	Dusts: Ready-to-use, easy application, high drift risk
•	Granules: Low drift, simple application, slow-release
•	Wettable Powders: Good residual activity, requires agitation
9.	Discuss the purpose and types of adjuvants in pesticide formulations Answer: Adjuvants are chemicals that improve pesticide performance by:
•	Enhancing spreading
•	Improving penetration
•	Reducing drift
•	Increasing stability
•	Modifying pH
10.	Identify special formulation types and their unique characteristics Answer:
•	Microencapsulated: Slow-release, enhanced safety
•	Water-Soluble Packaging: Reduced handler exposure
•	Impregnates: Controlled release in solid materials
•	Animal Systemics: Absorbed and distributed within animal tissues
Chapter 5 – Hazards
Key Terms to Know 📚
Toxicity and Exposure Terminology
1.	Toxicity: The ability of a pesticide to cause short-term (acute) or long-term (chronic) injury or illness.
2.	Exposure: The means by which a pesticide gets onto or into the body.
3.	Hazard (Risk): The potential for harm based on the combination of toxicity and exposure.
4.	Routes of Exposure:
•	Dermal (Skin)
•	Ocular (Eyes)
•	Inhalation (Lungs)
•	Oral (Mouth)
5.	Acute Toxicity: Harmful effects occurring within 24 hours of a single exposure.
6.	Chronic Toxicity: Harmful effects from repeated, prolonged exposure over time.
Medical and Physiological Terminology
1.	Cholinesterase: An enzyme necessary for proper nerve impulse transmission.
2.	Systemic Effects: Damage to organ systems after a pesticide is absorbed and distributed throughout the body.
3.	Local Effects: Injury at the point of contact with the body.
4.	Allergic Effects: Hypersensitive reactions to specific substances.
Signal Word Terminology
1.	DANGER-POISON: Indicates highest acute toxicity level.
2.	DANGER: Indicates high acute toxicity or severe contact hazards.
3.	WARNING: Indicates moderate toxicity.
4.	CAUTION: Indicates low toxicity.
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Explain the relationship between toxicity, exposure, and hazard Answer: Hazard = Toxicity × Exposure. The risk of harm depends on both the pesticide's inherent toxicity and the likelihood and extent of exposure.
2.	Describe the four primary routes of pesticide exposure Answer:
•	Dermal: Through the skin (most common route)
•	Ocular: Through the eyes
•	Inhalation: Through the lungs
•	Oral: Through the mouth
3.	Distinguish between acute and chronic toxicity Answer:
•	Acute Toxicity: Immediate effects from a single, large exposure
•	Chronic Toxicity: Long-term effects from repeated, small exposures
4.	Explain the significance of cholinesterase in pesticide exposure Answer: Cholinesterase is crucial for nerve impulse transmission. Organophosphate and carbamate insecticides inhibit this enzyme, potentially causing serious health problems affecting muscle function, including heart and breathing muscles.
5.	Interpret signal words and their implications Answer:
•	DANGER-POISON: Extremely toxic, potential for death
•	DANGER: Severe contact or systemic hazards
•	WARNING: Moderate toxicity
•	CAUTION: Low toxicity
6.	Identify the different types of pesticide effects Answer:
•	Local Effects: Injury at point of contact
•	Systemic Effects: Damage to internal organ systems
•	Allergic Effects: Hypersensitive reactions
•	Delayed Effects: Symptoms appearing long after exposure
7.	Describe first aid procedures for different types of pesticide exposure Answer:
•	Skin Exposure: Remove contaminated clothing, wash with soap and water
•	Eye Exposure: Flush with water for 15 minutes
•	Inhalation: Move to fresh air, perform artificial respiration if needed
•	Oral Exposure: Follow label instructions, do not induce vomiting unless directed
8.	Explain the importance of heat stress in pesticide handling Answer: Heat stress can:
•	Mimic pesticide poisoning symptoms
•	Increase risk of exposure
•	Cause serious health complications
•	Require careful monitoring and prevention
9.	Discuss the role of medical monitoring in pesticide handling Answer:
•	Regular medical checkups
•	Cholinesterase level monitoring
•	Early symptom recognition
•	Preventing long-term health effects
10.	List key emergency resources for pesticide exposure Answer:
•	Poison Control Center: 1-800-222-1222
•	National Pesticide Information Center: 1-800-858-7378
•	Animal Poison Control Center: 1-888-426-4435

Chapter 6 – PPE 
Key Terms to Know 📚
PPE Terminology
1.	Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Clothing and devices worn to protect the body from pesticide exposure.
2.	Chemical-Resistant: Materials that prevent measurable movement of chemicals through them.
3.	Dermal Exposure: Pesticide contact with skin.
4.	Respirator: A device that protects the user from inhaling contaminated air.
PPE Classification
1.	Tight-Fitting Respirator: A respirator that forms a seal directly on the face.
2.	Powered Air-Purifying Respirator (PAPR): A respirator using a blower to pass contaminated air through purifying elements.
3.	Qualitative Fit Test: A method using a test agent to check for respirator seal leakage.
4.	Quantitative Fit Test: A method using instrumentation to measure respirator leakage numerically.
Protection Terminology
1.	Routes of Exposure: Pathways through which pesticides can enter the body (dermal, inhalation, ocular, oral).
2.	Action Threshold: Pest population level at which control measures must be taken.
3.	Economic Threshold: Pest population density requiring control to prevent economic injury.
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Define Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and its importance Answer: PPE comprises clothing and devices worn to protect the body from pesticide contact, reducing the risk of injury, illness, or poisoning by minimizing exposure through various routes.
2.	Explain the minimum PPE requirements for pesticide handling Answer:
•	Long-sleeved shirt
•	Long pants
•	Closed-toed shoes
•	Socks
•	Additional PPE based on pesticide label requirements
3.	Describe the different types of respirators and their uses Answer:
•	Air-Purifying Respirators: Remove contaminants from air
•	Powered Air-Purifying Respirators (PAPR): Use blower to pass air through purifying elements
•	Atmosphere-Supplying Respirators: Provide clean air from an uncontaminated source
4.	Explain the importance of respirator fit testing Answer:
•	Ensures proper respirator seal
•	Prevents pesticide exposure
•	Identifies potential leakage
•	Requires annual testing or when physical changes occur
5.	List the key considerations for selecting appropriate gloves Answer:
•	Chemical resistance
•	Glove material
•	Thickness
•	Durability
•	Task-specific protection
•	Manufacturer recommendations
6.	Discuss the maintenance and care of PPE Answer:
•	Clean after each use
•	Inspect for damage
•	Store in clean, dry place
•	Replace when worn or contaminated
•	Follow manufacturer guidelines
7.	Identify the significance of different signal words on PPE labels Answer:
•	DANGER-POISON: Highest toxicity
•	DANGER: High toxicity or severe hazards
•	WARNING: Moderate toxicity
•	CAUTION: Low toxicity
8.	Explain the proper procedures for putting on and removing PPE Answer:
•	Put on PPE before exposure
•	Perform user seal checks for respirators
•	Remove PPE carefully to prevent contamination
•	Wash hands and body after removal
•	Launder PPE separately
9.	Describe the role of additional protective clothing Answer:
•	Coveralls: Second layer of protection
•	Aprons: Protect during mixing and loading
•	Headgear: Prevent overhead exposure
•	Footwear: Protect feet and prevent pesticide tracking
10.	Discuss the importance of personal hygiene in pesticide handling Answer:
•	Wash hands frequently
•	Avoid eating, drinking, or smoking
•	Shower after work
•	Change clothes
•	Clean and maintain PPE

Chapter 7 – Environment 
Key Terms to Know 📚
Environmental Interaction Terminology
1.	Drift: Movement of pesticides through air to non-target areas.
2.	Runoff: Movement of pesticides in surface water.
3.	Leaching: Downward movement of pesticides through soil into groundwater.
4.	Point-Source Pollution: Contamination from a specific, identifiable location.
5.	Nonpoint-Source Pollution: Contamination from a widespread area.
Pesticide Characteristic Terminology
1.	Solubility: Ability of a pesticide to dissolve in a solvent, typically water.
2.	Adsorption: Process of pesticide binding to soil particles.
3.	Persistence: Ability of a pesticide to remain active in its original form.
4.	Half-Life: Time needed for half of a pesticide to break down.
5.	Volatility: Tendency of a pesticide to turn into a gas or vapor.
Breakdown Terminology
1.	Chemical Degradation: Breakdown of chemicals without living organisms.
2.	Microbial Action: Breakdown by soil microorganisms.
3.	Photodegradation: Breakdown of chemicals due to sunlight exposure.
Learning Objectives and Brief Answers 🎯
1.	Explain how pesticides move in the environment Answer: Pesticides can move through air (drift), water (runoff and leaching), soil, and on/in objects, potentially contaminating non-target areas.
2.	Describe the key characteristics that influence pesticide environmental behavior Answer:
•	Solubility: Determines water movement
•	Adsorption: Affects soil binding
•	Persistence: Indicates breakdown rate
•	Volatility: Determines gaseous movement
3.	Discuss the sources and prevention of water contamination Answer:
•	Point-source: Specific locations like spills
•	Nonpoint-source: Widespread areas like agricultural fields
•	Prevention: Best management practices, careful application, buffer zones
4.	Explain the process of pesticide breakdown Answer: Pesticides break down through:
•	Chemical degradation
•	Microbial action
•	Photodegradation Factors like water and temperature influence breakdown speed.
5.	Define and describe pesticide drift Answer: Drift is the movement of pesticides through air, which can occur as:
•	Spray drift (liquid droplets)
•	Vapor drift (gaseous movement)
•	Particle drift (solid particles)
6.	Identify factors affecting pesticide leaching Answer: Leaching is influenced by:
•	Soil texture and structure
•	Organic matter content
•	Depth to groundwater
•	Geological characteristics
7.	Discuss the impact of pesticides on non-target organisms Answer: Pesticides can harm:
•	Non-target plants
•	Bees and beneficial insects
•	Fish and wildlife
•	Livestock
•	Endangered species
8.	Explain best management practices (BMPs) for minimizing environmental impact Answer:
•	Use Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
•	Identify vulnerable areas
•	Avoid mixing near water sources
•	Improve land use methods
•	Time applications carefully
•	Select products wisely
•	Handle pesticides safely
9.	Describe the role of the EPA in protecting the environment from pesticides Answer:
•	Regulates pesticide registration
•	Requires environmental hazard statements
•	Develops endangered species protection programs
•	Monitors and reviews environmental effects
10.	Discuss strategies for protecting sensitive areas Answer:
•	Create buffer zones
•	Avoid application near: 
•	Water sources
•	Schools
•	Playgrounds
•	Wildlife habitats
•	Endangered species locations
•	Use lowest effective pesticide concentration
•	Follow label instructions precisely

Safety Data Sheets
🔑 Key Terms to Know
1.	OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration)
•	Definition: The regulatory agency responsible for workplace safety
2.	HCS (Hazard Communication Standard)
•	Definition: OSHA's standard requiring chemical manufacturers, distributors, and importers to provide SDSs
3.	SDS (Safety Data Sheet)
•	Definition: A document providing detailed information about a hazardous chemical
4.	Uniform Format
•	Definition: The standardized 16-section format for Safety Data Sheets implemented on June 1, 2015
📚 Learning Objectives
Objective 1: Understand the Purpose of Safety Data Sheets
•	Brief Answer: SDSs are critical documents that communicate comprehensive information about hazardous chemicals to ensure workplace safety, providing detailed insights into chemical properties, potential hazards, and safe handling practices.
Objective 2: Comprehend the 16 SDS Sections
•	Brief Answer: Each of the 16 sections serves a specific purpose, covering everything from chemical identification and hazard classification to first aid measures, ecological information, and disposal considerations.
Objective 3: Recognize Key Information in Critical SDS Sections
•	Brief Answer: Focus on sections like: 
•	Section 2 (Hazard Identification): Understand chemical hazards and label elements
•	Section 4 (First-Aid Measures): Know immediate response procedures
•	Section 8 (Exposure Controls/Personal Protection): Identify necessary safety equipment
•	Section 11 (Toxicological Information): Understand potential health impacts
Objective 4: Identify Regulatory Responsibilities
•	Brief Answer: Understand that different agencies (OSHA, EPA) oversee various aspects of chemical safety, with OSHA primarily focusing on workplace safety sections of the SDS.
🌟 Pro Tips
•	Always read the entire SDS before working with a new chemical
•	Pay special attention to Section 2 (Hazards) and Section 8 (Personal Protection)
•	Use the SDS as a critical reference for emergency situations
