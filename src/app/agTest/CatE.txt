Category E
E01 – Introduction
Key Terms to Know 📚
1.	Pesticide Applicator
•	Definition: A professional certified to apply pesticides in specific settings like turf, ornamental landscapes, and green industries
2.	Category E: Turf and Ornamental
•	Definition: A pesticide license category covering pesticide application in non-agricultural areas such as lawns, parks, golf courses, nurseries, and greenhouses
3.	Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
•	Definition: A strategy for pest control that balances environmental concerns, pest control effectiveness, and minimal chemical intervention
4.	Invasive Species
•	Definition: Non-native plants or insects that can harm native plant communities, such as Emerald Ash Borer or Buckthorn
5.	Closed-Book Exam
•	Definition: A certification test where no reference materials are allowed during the examination

E02 Environment
Key Terms and Definitions 📚
1.	Adjuvant
•	Definition: An additive to a pesticide that enhances pesticide effectiveness
2.	Aquatic Life
•	Definition: Organisms that spend all or part of their life in surface waters
3.	Direct Pesticide Application
•	Definition: A precise application to a specific area or site, such as a basal application to woody plants or crack-and-crevice treatment
4.	Indirect Pesticide Applications
•	Definition: Unintentional or intentional treatment of pests through spraying or placement of a pesticide on a plant, rather than directly on the pest
5.	Leaching
•	Definition: The downward movement of pesticides and nutrients through the soil
6.	Non-target Species
•	Definition: Organisms not intended to be managed
7.	Runoff
•	Definition: The movement of water and associated materials over the top of the soil or impervious surfaces
8.	Toxicity
•	Definition: The extent to which a chemical or substance is poisonous
9.	Transformation
•	Definition: Change in a pesticide's chemical structure
10.	Transportation
•	Definition: The movement of a pesticide away from its intended site of action (off-site)
Learning Objectives and Answers 🎯
1.	Objective: List four ways pesticides are transported in the environment
•	Answers: a. Runoff b. Leaching c. Particle Drift d. Volatilization Drift
2.	Objective: List four ways pesticides are transformed in the environment
•	Answers: a. Absorption b. Photodecomposition c. Microbial Decomposition d. Chemical Breakdown
3.	Objective: List three ways to control pests while preventing negative environmental impacts
•	Answers: a. Use Integrated Pest Management (IPM) b. Choose least toxic pesticides c. Apply pesticides carefully and precisely
4.	Objective: Describe three ways pesticides may negatively impact the environment
•	Answers: a. Reduction of pollinator populations b. Contamination of ground and surface water c. Harm to non-target organisms
5.	Objective: List cultural practices that can reduce pesticide use
•	Answers: a. Choose site-appropriate plants b. Manage soil health c. Use proper turfgrass management techniques d. Implement preventative landscape design
E03 Plant Biology
Key Terms to Know 📖
Plant Classification Terms
1.	Annual: A plant that completes its life cycle in one year
2.	Biennial: A plant that requires two growing seasons to complete its life cycle
3.	Perennial: A plant that survives from year to year
4.	Monocot: Plants with one seed leaf (cotyledon)
5.	Dicot: Plants with two seed leaves (cotyledons)
6.	Gymnosperms: Non-flowering plants (like conifers)
7.	Angiosperms: Flowering plants
Plant Structure Terms
1.	Meristem: Zones of actively dividing cells where growth occurs
2.	Xylem: Tissue that conducts water and dissolved minerals
3.	Phloem: Tissue that transports carbohydrates and organic compounds
4.	Rhizomes: Horizontal underground stems that produce shoots and roots
5.	Adventitious Roots: Roots that form from stems or injuries
Growth and Reproduction Terms
1.	Photosynthesis: Process of converting light energy into chemical energy
2.	Respiration: Process of converting food into usable energy
3.	Sexual Reproduction: Reproduction involving pollen fertilizing pistils
4.	Asexual Reproduction: Formation of new individuals from a single parent's cells
Learning Objectives 🎯
Objective 1: List the Six Basic Requirements for Plant Growth
Answer:
•	Light
•	Water
•	Temperature
•	Oxygen
•	Carbon Dioxide
•	Nutrients
Objective 2: Identify Plant Parts for Weed and Cultivated Plant Identification
Answer:
•	Stems (nodes, internodes, buds)
•	Leaves (blade, petiole, stipules)
•	Roots (root systems, rhizomes)
•	Flowers
•	Seed structures
Objective 3: Describe Pesticide Transportation Within Plants
Answer:
•	Pesticides enter through roots and leaves
•	Xylem transports herbicides from roots to shoots
•	Phloem translocates herbicides to meristems
•	Fall applications are most effective for perennial plants
Objective 4: Explain How Leaf Structure Affects Pesticide Uptake
Answer:
•	Leaf surface area impacts herbicide penetration
•	Waxy cuticles and leaf hairs can minimize herbicide absorption
•	Surfactants may improve leaf absorption
•	Leaf shape and structure determine herbicide effectiveness
Objective 5: Differentiate Between Annuals, Biennials, and Perennials
Answer:
•	Annuals: Complete life cycle in one year
•	Biennials: Require two growing seasons (vegetative growth first year, flowering second year)
•	Perennials: Live multiple years (can be herbaceous or woody)
Objective 6: Identify Meristem Locations
Answer:
•	Apical meristems: At stem and root tips
•	Secondary meristems: Responsible for stem diameter growth
•	Marginal meristems: Near nodes and leaf attachments
Objective 7: Explain Meristem Susceptibility to Chemical Damage
Answer:
•	Meristems are actively growing areas
•	Systemic herbicides target growing points
•	Contact herbicides must thoroughly cover the plant
•	Damage to meristems can halt plant growth and development

E05 Insects & Mites
Key Terms and Definitions 📚
Arthropod
•	Definition: An invertebrate characterized by a segmented body, jointed limbs, and a hard exoskeleton that is periodically molted
•	Examples: Insects, mites, crayfish
Borers
•	Definition: Insects that feed internally within the trunks, branches, or roots of plants
Gall
•	Definition: An abnormal growth on plant tissue (leaf, stem, or twig) induced by a gall-making organism
Insects
•	Definition: Arthropods distinguished by a three-part body (head, thorax, abdomen), three pairs of legs, and typically four wings
Metamorphosis, Complete
•	Definition: An insect life cycle consisting of four distinct stages: egg, larva, pupa, and adult, where the larval stage differs significantly from the adult form
Metamorphosis, Incomplete
•	Definition: An insect life cycle consisting of three stages: egg, nymph, and adult, where nymphs resemble smaller, wingless versions of the adults
Parasitoids
•	Definition: Insects that deposit their eggs on or inside a host insect; the emerging larvae gradually consume and eventually kill the host
Pollinators
•	Definition: Insects that facilitate the transfer of pollen between flowers
Predators
•	Definition: Insects that capture and consume other insects or organisms
Learning Objectives 🎯
Objective 1: Identify and Describe Five Types of Insect Pests
Answer:
1.	Leaf-chewing insects (e.g., caterpillars, sawflies)
2.	Sucking insects (e.g., aphids, lace bugs)
3.	Root-feeding insects (e.g., white grubs, billbugs)
4.	Shoot, stem, branch, and trunk borers
5.	Gall-forming insects and mites
Objective 2: Distinguish Between Two Primary Insect Life Cycles
Answer:
1.	Complete Metamorphosis: Four stages (egg, larva, pupa, adult)
•	Larval stage looks very different from adult
•	Examples: beetles, moths, butterflies, flies
2.	Incomplete Metamorphosis: Three stages (egg, nymph, adult)
•	Nymphs look similar to adults
•	Examples: grasshoppers, thrips, true bugs
Objective 3: Enumerate Five Strategies for Managing Insect Populations
Answer:
1.	No Action (when damage is minimal)
2.	Genetic (using resistant plant varieties)
3.	Cultural (maintaining plant health)
4.	Biological Control (encouraging natural predators)
5.	Pesticide Application (using targeted, low-impact methods)

E06 Weeds
Key Terms 🌿
1.	Weed
•	Definition: A plant out of place, or an unwanted plant
•	Detailed Explanation: Any plant that interferes with desired aesthetic or growth of cultivated plants
2.	Noxious Weeds
•	Definition: Plants identified by Minnesota Statute as harmful to public health, environment, crops, livestock, and property
•	Categories: a) Prohibited Noxious Weeds b) Restricted Noxious Weeds c) Specially Regulated Noxious Plants
3.	Invasive Species
•	Definition: Plants that are not native and can cause economic, environmental, or health harm
4.	Allelopathy
•	Definition: Production of growth inhibitors by some weeds that retard development of desirable plants
5.	Vegetative Propagation
•	Definition: Asexual plant reproduction where new plants grow from underground structures or cuttings
6.	Herbicide Types
•	Preemergent: Prevents weed seed germination
•	Postemergent: Applied after plant emerges
•	Selective: Targets specific plant types
•	Non-selective: Kills most plants
•	Contact: Kills only treated plant parts
•	Systemic: Absorbed and moved throughout the plant
Learning Objectives 📚
1. List the Five Categories of Weeds
Answer:
1.	Grasses
2.	Sedges
3.	Mosses/Algae
4.	Herbaceous Broadleaves
5.	Woody Broadleaves
2. Identify Importance of Indicator Weeds
Answer:
•	Weeds can indicate specific soil or environmental problems
•	Their presence helps diagnose underlying issues like: 
•	Soil pH
•	Compaction
•	Moisture levels
•	Fertility
•	Correcting these conditions can minimize weed growth
3. List Characteristics for Herbicide Selection
Answer:
1.	Preemergent or Postemergent
2.	Selective or Non-selective
3.	Contact or Systemic
4.	Mode of Action
5.	Amine or Ester Formulation
6.	Single or Combination Product
7.	With or Without Adjuvant
4. Describe Weed Damage
Answer:
•	Compete for resources (water, nutrients, light, space)
•	Interfere with management practices
•	Produce growth inhibitors
•	Can be toxic or irritating
•	Outcompete native species
•	Reduce aesthetic value of landscapes
5. Weed Control Growth Stages
Answer:
•	Annual Weeds: Most susceptible when small and immature
•	Perennial Weeds: Best controlled in fall for root system translocation
•	Specific timing varies by weed type and life cycle
6. Minnesota Noxious Weed Information Location
Answer:
•	Minnesota Department of Agriculture (MDA) Website
•	Link: http://www.mda.state.mn.us/plants/badplants/noxiouslist.aspx

E07 Disease
Key Terms and Definitions 📚
Biological Terms
1.	Bacteria
•	Definition: Single-celled microbes that reproduce by dividing
2.	Disorder
•	Definition: Reduced growth due to abiotic (non-living) factors
3.	Disease
•	Definition: Reduced growth due to plant pathogens
4.	Fungi
•	Definition: Organisms growing in filaments, reproducing by spores, and incapable of producing their own food
5.	Host
•	Definition: A plant providing food and living conditions for a parasite
6.	Nematodes
•	Definition: Tiny non-segmented roundworms
7.	Pathogen
•	Definition: Disease-causing agent
8.	Phytoplasmas
•	Definition: Small bacteria lacking a cell wall
9.	Virus
•	Definition: Microscopic parasite with genetic material and a protein coating
Learning Objectives 🎯
Objective 1: Identify Conditions Causing Plant Diseases or Disorders
Answer:
•	Abiotic Factors: Non-living environmental conditions 
•	Examples: Improper watering, nutrient imbalances, temperature extremes
•	Biotic Factors: Living organisms causing disease 
•	Examples: Fungi, bacteria, viruses, nematodes, phytoplasmas
Objective 2: List Types of Plant Pathogens
Answer:
1.	Fungi
2.	Bacteria
3.	Viruses
4.	Phytoplasmas
5.	Nematodes
Objective 3: List Four Disease Management Strategies
Answer:
1.	Cultural Control
•	Modify environmental conditions
•	Improve plant health and resistance
2.	Sanitation
•	Remove infected plant parts
•	Prevent disease spread
3.	Chemical Control
•	Use of fungicides, bactericides

4.	Genetic Control

E09 Equipment
📚 Key Terms
1.	Calibration
•	Definition: The practice of verifying and/or adjusting fertilizer or pesticide application equipment to accurately apply a predetermined amount of product per area unit.
2.	Misapplication
•	Definition: Placement of a pesticide or fertilizer beyond the target area or in improper amounts.
3.	Tree Injection
•	Definition: Treatment of trees with pesticides by delivering systemic pesticides (usually insecticides) through a hole drilled into the active xylem of the tree.
4.	Sprayer Overlap
•	Definition: Intentional application on top of a previous application to account for reduced volumes of material applied at the edges and ensure adequate coverage.
5.	Volume per Unit Area
•	Definition: Amount of material applied over a set area.
🎯 Learning Objectives and Answers
1.	Objective: Explain the importance of calibrating application equipment
•	Answer: Calibration ensures accurate application rates, preventing over or under-application, which can lead to ineffective pest control, plant damage, or environmental harm.
2.	Objective: Explain proper techniques for calibrating application equipment
•	Answer: Techniques involve measuring output over a known area, adjusting settings, and re-measuring until the desired rate is achieved. This includes checking nozzle output, travel speed, and maintaining consistent pressure.
3.	Objective: Explain the relationship between equipment speed and application rate
•	Answer: Application rate is inversely proportional to speed. Faster speed reduces the application rate, while slower speed increases the application rate.
4.	Objective: Explain the relationship between pressure and application rate
•	Answer: Application rate is directly proportional to pressure. Increasing pressure increases the application rate, while decreasing pressure reduces the application rate.
5.	Objective: Calculate areas of rectangle, triangle, and circle
•	Formulas: 
•	Rectangle: A=l×w
•	Triangle: A=21×b×h
•	Circle: A=π×r2
6.	Objective: List three key considerations for calibration check
•	Answer: 
1.	Nozzle flow rate
2.	Travel speed
3.	Swath width
7.	Objective: List three warning signs of misapplication
•	Answer: 
1.	Uneven coverage (streaking or missed areas)
2.	Visible drift
3.	Phytotoxicity symptoms on non-target plants
8.	Objective: Identify six major types of pesticide application equipment
•	Answer: 
1.	Low-pressure sprayers
2.	High-pressure sprayers
3.	Backpack sprayers
4.	Ride-on sprayers
5.	Pull-behind sprayers
6.	Air-blast sprayers
9.	Objective: Identify when to complete equipment maintenance
•	Answer: Before each use, during use (checking for leaks or clogs), and after each use (cleaning and storing). Regular, scheduled maintenance should also be performed.
10.	Objective: List three pesticide application techniques used in greenhouses
•	Answer: 
1.	Spot treatment with spray guns
2.	Wick applicators
3.	Shielded applicators
E10 Safety
Key Terms and Definitions 📚
1.	Professionalism
•	Definition: The skill, good judgment, and polite behavior expected from a person who is trained to do a job well
2.	Sensitive Sites
•	Definition: Properties or areas that are particularly vulnerable to harm from pesticide exposure
•	Examples: 
•	Schools
•	Daycare centers
•	Healthcare facilities
•	Playgrounds
•	Recreation areas
3.	Chemigation
•	Definition: The application of pesticides through irrigation systems
4.	Reentry Date
•	Definition: The period after pesticide application during which entry to the treated area is restricted
5.	Worker Protection Standard (WPS)
•	Definition: A federal regulation designed to protect employees when pesticides are used in agricultural settings
Learning Objectives and Answers 🎯
1.	Objective: Identify common pesticide safety risks for turf and ornamental applicators and how to minimize those risks
•	Answer: 
•	Wear appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
•	Schedule work to minimize exposure
•	Be aware of co-workers' locations
•	Follow label instructions
•	Use extra caution during overhead applications
2.	Objective: Identify common pesticide safety risks for customers and the public and how to minimize those risks
•	Answer: 
•	Post warning signs
•	Communicate application times
•	Block off treated areas
•	Ensure people and pets stay away during and after application
•	Provide information about reentry intervals
3.	Objective: Describe technical and ethical standards of professionalism

•	Follow all regulations and label instructions
•	Prioritize safety over profit
•	Communicate clearly with customers
•	Continuously educate yourself
•	Make informed, responsible decisions
4.	Objective: List four aspects of professionalism

1.	Ethical standards
2.	Knowing and following health and safety regulations
3.	Effective communication
4.	Good judgment
5.	Objective: List where pesticide application posting requirements may be found
