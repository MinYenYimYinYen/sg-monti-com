•	Reference: Page 1, 3-4

•	Reference: Page 1

•	Answers: 
•	Protection of public health and the environment
•	Keeping pesticides secured
•	Concerns about pesticide misapplication
•	Preventing harm to applicators, coworkers, family, and customers
•	Avoiding contamination of water, food, land, and air
2.	Describe the goal of the pesticide applicator licensing program
•	Answer: To license qualified applicators who meet recognized health, safety, environmental, security, and pest management standards through certification via testing and education
3.	List five examples of pesticide misuse

1.	Not using a pesticide according to label directions
2.	Using a pesticide not registered for sale or use in Minnesota
3.	Applying pesticide in a manner that endangers people or animals
4.	Allowing drift and off-target site movement of pesticides
5.	Applying a pesticide without holding the required pesticide applicator license

AS02 – Licensing 
Key Terms 📚
1.	Restricted Use Pesticides (RUPs)
•	Definition: Pesticide formulations designated by FIFRA that can only be applied by licensed or certified applicators in Minnesota.
2.	Non-Restricted Use Pesticides (Non-RUPs)
•	Definition: All pesticides other than RUPs, including general use, unclassified pesticides, organic pesticides, and EPA's 25(b) minimum risk pesticides.
3.	Commercial Pesticide Applicator
•	Definition: Applicators who apply pesticides "for hire" to customer's land or sites for payment or fee.
4.	Noncommercial Pesticide Applicator
•	Definition: Employees who apply pesticides to their employer's land or sites.
5.	Structural Pest Control Applicator (SPCA)
•	Definition: Applicators who apply pesticides "for hire" in, on, or around customer's buildings and structures.
6.	Private Pesticide Applicator
•	Definition: Individuals who apply pesticides on their own land or sites for agricultural commodity production.
7.	Certification
•	Definition: The testing process by which the Minnesota Department of Agriculture determines an applicator meets required competency standards.
8.	Licensing
•	Definition: The process of issuing credentials that provide individuals access to pesticides registered for use in Minnesota.
9.	Recertification
•	Definition: The process of maintaining certification by attending workshops or retesting in specific categories.
Learning Objectives 🎯
Objective 1: List the Three Broad Purposes of the Pesticide Licensing System
Answer:
1.	Control access to Restricted Use Pesticides (RUPs) through licensing to those who have demonstrated competency.
2.	Limit category-specific pesticide applications through licensing.
3.	Ensure licensed individuals receive continuing education on laws, regulations, best practices, and pest management information.


Objective 2: List Ways Minnesota's Licensing Requirements Differ from Other States
Answer:
1.	Requires all RUPs to be applied by licensed applicators.
2.	Requires licensing for commercial pesticide applications of both RUPs and non-RUPs.
3.	Requires licensed applicators for all pesticide applications on golf courses.
4.	Only allows EPA-designated 25(b) or Minnesota-registered pesticide products.
5.	Does not allow unlicensed applicators to apply RUPs, even under supervision.
6.	No minimum age requirement for obtaining a pesticide license.
7.	Licenses are not transferable between individuals or employers.
Objective 3: Describe When Commercial or Noncommercial Pesticide Applicator Licenses are Required
Answer:
•	Commercial Applicator: Required for any pesticide application done "for hire", including RUPs and non-RUPs.
•	Noncommercial Applicator: Required to apply RUPs on employer's land, and for all pesticide applications on golf courses.
Objective 4: Describe When a Structural Pest Control Applicator License is Required
Answer:
•	Required for pesticide applications "for hire" in, on, or around buildings and structures.
•	Must hold either an SPCA Journeyman or SPCA Master license.
•	Needed to control structural pests and apply any pesticide (RUP or non-RUP).
Objective 5: List General Steps for Obtaining an Initial Pesticide Applicator License
Answer:
1.	Complete the applicator license form.
2.	Pay the license application fee.
3.	Verify identity with official photo ID.
4.	Pass required exams with 70% or higher score.
5.	Receive license ID card from Minnesota Department of Agriculture.
Objective 6: List Recertification Requirements
Answer:
1.	Recertify within the year following initial certification.
2.	Two recertification methods: 
•	Attend MDA-approved category-specific recertification workshops.
•	Retest by taking category certification exams.
3.	Recertification frequency varies by category (1-3 years).
Objective 7: Find License Expiration and Recertification Dates
Answer:
•	All licenses expire on December 31st each year.
•	Recertification date is listed on the license ID card for each category.

AS03 – Records
Learning Objectives and Answers 🎯
1.	Objective: Describe which pesticide applications require an applicator to make and keep a record.
•	Answer: 
•	Noncommercial applicators must keep records for Restricted Use Pesticide (RUP) applications
•	Commercial and Structural (SPCA) applicators must keep records for ALL pesticide applications (both RUP and non-RUP)
2.	Objective: List how the recordkeeping requirements differ for commercial, noncommercial, and structural applicators.
•	Answer: 
•	Noncommercial Applicators: 
•	Record only RUP applications
•	No requirement to provide customer records
•	Commercial Applicators: 
•	Record ALL pesticide applications
•	Must provide complete records to customers
•	Structural Applicators: 
•	Record ALL pesticide applications
•	Must provide complete records to customers
3.	Objective: Explain where to find information about required pesticide application records.
•	Answer: 
•	Minnesota Department of Agriculture (MDA) website
•	Navigate to 'Pesticide Application Records (Samples)' section
4.	Objective: Describe environmental information recording for pesticide applications.
•	Answer: 
•	Required for outdoor applications
•	Must record: 
•	Actual wind speed
•	Wind direction
•	Temperature
5.	Objective: State who must keep records and how long they must be retained.
•	Answer: 
•	Who: 
•	Applicator
•	Company
•	Authorized agent
•	Retention Period: 5 years after the date of treatment
6.	Objective: Describe how soon a record must be completed after an application.
•	Answer: 
•	Must be completed "as soon as possible" following the application
•	Ideally immediately after application or by the end of the work day
Key Terms Glossary 📚
1.	Licensed Applicator:
•	A person certified to apply pesticides, with specific recordkeeping requirements
2.	Restricted Use Pesticide (RUP):
•	Pesticides that can only be purchased and applied by certified applicators
3.	Commercial Applicator:
•	An applicator who applies pesticides for hire
4.	Noncommercial Applicator:
•	An applicator who applies pesticides on property they own or control, not for hire
5.	Structural Pest Control Applicator (SPCA):
•	An applicator specializing in pest control for structures
6.	Environmental Conditions:
•	Factors like wind speed, direction, and temperature that impact pesticide application
7.	Record Retention:
•	The requirement to keep pesticide application records for a specified period (5 years in this case)
8.	Authorized Agent:
•	A person authorized to maintain records on behalf of the applicator or company

AS04 – Endangered Species
📘 Key Terms and Definitions
1. Endangered Species
•	Definition: A plant or animal species in danger of extinction
•	Example: Minnesota dwarf trout lily (Erythronium propullans)
2. Threatened Species
•	Definition: A plant or animal species likely to become endangered in the foreseeable future
•	Example: Leedy's roseroot (Sedum integrifolium ssp. leedyi)
3. Candidate Species
•	Definition: Plants or animals being considered for designation as threatened or endangered
•	Example: Sprague's pipit (Anthus spragueii)
🎯 Learning Objectives
Objective 1: Describe What is Illegal Regarding Endangered or Threatened Species
Answers:
•	Killing or harming an endangered or threatened species
•	Destroying or modifying critical habitats
•	Digging up, removing, or harming listed endangered or threatened plants
•	Importing, transporting, or selling any part of a listed species
Objective 2: How to Find Out About Endangered, Threatened, or Candidate Species
Answers:
1.	Check resources:
•	Minnesota DNR website (www.dnr.state.mn.us/ets)
•	US Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) lists
•	EPA's Bulletins Live! System (www.epa.gov/espp/bulletins.htm)
2.	Contact agencies:
•	Minnesota DNR: (651) 296-6157 or (888) 646-6367
•	US Fish and Wildlife Service: (612) 725-3548
3.	Communicate with landowners about protected species presence
AS05 Water
📘 Key Terms and Definitions
1.	Pesticide Management Plan
•	A state-level strategy to protect groundwater and surface water from pesticide contamination
•	Focuses on preventing pesticides and their breakdown products from reaching unsafe levels in water resources
2.	Best Management Practices (BMPs)
•	Research-based, practical methods to prevent or minimize contamination
•	Includes activities like storing, transporting, mixing, and loading pesticides
3.	Application Setbacks
•	The distance of untreated land between a pesticide-treated area and areas needing protection
•	Helps prevent pesticide runoff into sensitive areas
4.	Filter Strips and Vegetative Buffers
•	Untreated grass strips next to streams, ponds, and sensitive areas
•	Trap pesticide runoff from treated sites
5.	Depth to Groundwater
•	The distance from the soil surface down to the water table
•	Important for understanding potential pesticide leaching risks
6.	Backflow
•	The undesired reversal of water flow, which can contaminate water sources with pesticides
•	Can occur when water pressure drops unexpectedly
7.	Air Gap
•	A physical separation between a water source and a container being filled
•	Prevents pesticide contamination during equipment filling
8.	Reduced Pressure Principle Device
•	A mechanical backflow prevention device with two check valves and a pressure differential valve
•	Prevents pesticides from backflowing into water sources
9.	Pressurized Vacuum Breaker
•	A device that stops backflow when water line pressure is lost
•	Installed in a vertical position on water supply lines
10.	Atmospheric Vacuum Breaker
•	Allows air into the water line to break the backflow siphon
•	Must be installed at least six inches above the overflow level
11.	Chemigation
•	The application of pesticides and fertilizers through irrigation systems
12.	Chemigation Operator
•	The legal entity (person or persons) involved in operating a chemigation system
🎯 Learning Objectives and Answers
1.	Objective: State which herbicides have voluntary Water Quality Best Management Practices
•	Answer: 
•	Acetochlor
•	Alachlor
•	Atrazine
•	Metolachlor
•	Metribuzin
2.	Objective: Describe how drinking water sources may be contaminated when filling pesticide application equipment
•	Answer: 
•	Backflow can occur if water pressure drops unexpectedly
•	Electricity shutdown or pump failure can cause siphoning of pesticides back into water sources
•	This can happen when filling equipment from private wells, city water systems, or surface waters
3.	Objective: List types of backflow prevention devices approved in Minnesota
•	Answer: 
1.	Air Gap
2.	Reduced Pressure Principle Device (RPP/RPZ)
3.	Pressurized Vacuum Breaker (PVB)
4.	Atmospheric Vacuum Breaker (AVB)
4.	Objective: Calculate air gap distance for water hoses above pesticide containers
•	Answer: 
•	Air gap must be at least two times the inside diameter of the water supply line
•	Example: For a 2-inch water supply line, the air gap must be at least 4 inches above the container rim
5.	Objective: Identify who should install mechanical backflow prevention devices
•	Answer: 
•	A licensed plumber who complies with the Minnesota Plumbing Code
•	Must contact local health department for specific installation specifications
6.	Objective: Describe backflow prevention requirements for rinsing pesticide containers
•	Answer: 
•	Air gap is NOT an option
•	Must use either: 
1.	Hose connection vacuum breaker on the faucet
2.	Double-check valve with intermediate atmospheric vent on water supply line
7.	Objective: List legal requirements for chemigation in Minnesota
•	Answer: 
•	Only use pesticides registered and labeled for chemigation
•	Obtain MDA Chemigation Permit
•	Install safety anti-pollution and backflow safeguard devices
•	Post chemigated treated areas
•	Follow setbacks and containment requirements
•	Keep records of applied pesticides and fertilizers
8.	Objective: Identify who needs a chemigation permit
•	Answer: 
•	All operators applying pesticides through irrigation systems
•	Includes the legal entity (person or persons) involved in system operation, maintenance, and use

AS06 Disposal
📚 Key Terms and Definitions
1. Pesticide Waste
•	Definition: Pesticides that cannot be used as originally intended, including: 
•	Old pesticides that may no longer be effective
•	Pesticides you no longer need or want
•	Pesticide containers and other contaminated materials
2. Waste Pesticides
•	Definition: Pesticides that are no longer usable or wanted due to: 
•	Government ban
•	Loss of effectiveness from storage
•	Leftover or unwanted quantities
3. Household Hazardous Waste Facility (HHW)
•	Definition: A designated facility for collecting and properly disposing of household hazardous waste, including certain types of pesticide waste
🎯 Learning Objectives and Answers
Objective 1: Reducing Waste Pesticide and Container Usage
Answer:
•	Buy only the amount of pesticides needed for one year
•	Use careful handling during mixing, loading, and application
•	Protect pesticides through good storage practices
•	Store leftover pesticides in original containers with labels
•	Keep all pesticides in a locked, secured area
Objective 2: Best Time to Rinse Pesticide Containers
Answer:
•	Rinse containers immediately when emptied
•	Prevents hardening of residues
•	Eliminates small spills and leakage
•	Ensures no visible pesticide residue remains
Objective 3: Legal Disposal Options
Answer: For Empty Containers:
•	Recycle through specific pesticide container recycling programs
•	Take to county solid waste offices
•	Do NOT burn or bury outside sanitary landfills
For Waste Pesticides:
•	Use according to label directions
•	Deliver to Waste Pesticide Collection
•	Take to participating county HHW facilities
Objective 4: Storage Requirements for Empty Containers
Answer:
•	Unrinsed Containers:
•	Store like containers with pesticide
•	Replace cap
•	Store upright in roofed, secure, locked area
•	Store on impervious surface
•	Pressure-Rinsed Containers:
•	Store indoors
•	Prevent water/dirt entry
•	Remove caps for complete drying
•	Triple-Rinsed Containers:
•	Can be stored outside
•	Replace caps
•	Keep dry and clean
•	No need for impervious surface
Objective 5: Preparing Empty Pesticide Containers for Recycling
Answer:
•	Remove caps
•	Peel off labels
•	Slide off plastic sleeves/wrappers
•	Discard removed materials as solid waste
•	Do NOT include in household recycling
•	Containers must be properly rinsed (triple or pressure rinsed)

AS07 Incident Response
📚 Key Terms and Definitions
1.	Incident
•	Definition: A flood, fire, tornado, transportation accident, storage container rupture, portable container rupture, leak, spill, emission, discharge, escape, disposal, or other event that: 
•	Releases or immediately threatens to release an agricultural chemical
•	Accidentally or intentionally occurs
•	May cause unreasonable adverse effects on the environment
2.	Incident Response Plan
•	Definition: A detailed document describing: 
•	Pesticide and fertilizer storage practices
•	Handling procedures
•	Disposal methods
•	Incident handling practices for a business
3.	Responsible Party
•	Definition: A person who, at the time of an incident, has: 
•	Custody of a pesticide or fertilizer
•	Control of a pesticide or fertilizer
•	Responsibility for a pesticide or fertilizer container or rinsate
4.	Agricultural Chemicals
•	Definition: Includes both: 
•	Fertilizers: Substances containing plant nutrients used to promote plant growth
•	Pesticides: Substances intended to prevent, destroy, repel, or mitigate pests
5.	STARR
•	Definition: Five-step incident response protocol: 
•	Secure
•	Telephone
•	Abate
•	Recover
•	Remediate
6.	ACRRA
•	Definition: Agricultural Chemical Response and Reimbursement Account - A fund providing partial reimbursement for cleanup costs of agricultural chemical incidents
🎯 Learning Objectives with Answers
1.	Who must develop and regularly update an incident response plan?
•	Answer: All businesses in Minnesota that: 
•	Make commercial, noncommercial, or structural pesticide applications
•	Store bulk pesticides or fertilizers (including anhydrous ammonia)
2.	Who should be called to report an agricultural chemical spill?
•	Answer: 
•	First, call 911 if there's a threat to public safety
•	Then, call the Minnesota State Duty Officer: 
•	1-800-422-0798 (Greater Minnesota)
•	(651) 649-5451 (Twin Cities Metropolitan area)
3.	What are seven potential causes of a pesticide incident?
•	Answer: 
1.	Improper storage and disposal
2.	Mixing and loading containment failures
3.	Transportation spills
4.	Application spills
5.	Equipment failure
6.	Vandalism
7.	Acts of Nature (floods, tornadoes, earthquakes)
4.	Who should call the State Duty Officer and when?
•	Answer: 
•	The "responsible party" or "owner of real property"
•	Immediately upon discovering a pesticide or fertilizer incident
•	Exceptions exist for very small, contained releases
5.	What are the steps in STARR for handling a pesticide incident?
•	Answer: 
1.	Secure: Keep people/animals away, wear PPE
2.	Telephone: Call 911 and State Duty Officer
3.	Abate: Control contamination source
4.	Recover: Collect released chemicals
5.	Remediate: Dispose of contaminated materials
6.	What are the key parts of an Incident Response Plan?
•	Answer: 
•	Emergency contact list
•	Product labels and MSDS
•	First aid information
•	Pre-fire planning
•	Facility maps
•	Handling procedures
•	Emergency equipment list
•	Release procedures
•	Employee training documentation
7.	Where should an Incident Response Plan be stored?
•	Answer: 
•	Prominent location at storage facility
•	Accessible to all employees
•	Backup copy at a different location
•	Copy in each service vehicle
•	Copies with local fire and police departments
8.	Who should be familiar with an Incident Response Plan?
•	Answer: All persons working with pesticides and fertilizers
9.	Why are pesticide labels and MSDS important in an Incident Response Plan?
•	Answer: They provide critical information for: 
•	Identifying chemicals
•	Understanding health and safety risks
•	Guiding emergency response actions

AS Terms Summary
Pesticide Applicator Certification: Key Terms Outline 🌱🔬
I. Water Protection and Backflow Prevention
•	Air Gap: Physical separation preventing water contamination
•	Atmospheric Vacuum Breaker: Device to break backflow siphon
•	Backflow: Siphoning of pesticides back into water source
•	Pressurized Vacuum Breaker: Stops backflow under continuous pressure
•	Reduced Pressure Principle Device (RPP/RPZ): Advanced backflow prevention
II. Regulatory Framework and Legal Definitions
Applicator Types
•	Commercial Applicator: Applies pesticides on customer's land
•	Noncommercial Applicator: Applies pesticides on employer's land
•	Private Pesticide Applicator: Applies pesticides on own/leased/rented land
•	Structural Pest Control Applicator (SPCA): Manages pests in/around buildings
Licensing and Certification
•	Certification: Testing process for competency
•	License Category: Based on pesticide use type
•	License Renewal: Continuing applicator license
•	Licensing: Credentialing process
•	Recertification: Attending workshops or retesting
•	Reciprocity: Cross-state license recognition
Regulatory Concepts
•	Agricultural Chemical Response and Reimbursement Account (ACRRA): Cleanup cost reimbursement
•	Agricultural Chemicals: Fertilizers and pesticides
•	Best Management Practices (BMPs): Voluntary contamination reduction practices
•	Incident: Unauthorized chemical release
•	Incident Response Plan: Chemical handling and emergency procedures
•	Pesticide Management Plan: Water resource protection program
•	Registered Products: Approved pesticides in Minnesota
•	Restricted Use Pesticides (RUPs): High-hazard pesticides
•	State Lead Agency: Implements pesticide laws
III. Environmental Protection
•	Application Setbacks: Untreated land buffer zones
•	Candidate Species: Potential threatened/endangered species
•	Depth to Groundwater: Distance to water table
•	Endangered Species: Species in danger of extinction
•	Filter Strips and Vegetative Buffers: Runoff trapping areas
•	Threatened Species: Likely to become endangered
IV. Pesticide Waste Management
•	Household Hazardous Waste Facility (HHW): Waste collection centers
•	Pesticide Wastes: Unusable or unwanted pesticides
•	Waste Pesticides: No longer usable chemicals
V. Special Application Methods
•	Chemigation: Pesticide application through irrigation systems
•	Chemigation Operator: System operation manager
VI. Incident Response
•	STARR: 
1.	Secure the area
2.	Telephone authorities
3.	Abate contamination source
4.	Recover chemicals
5.	Remediate materials
VII. Additional Classifications
•	Non-Restricted Use Pesticides (Non-RUPs): General use pesticides
•	Pesticide Misuse: Illegal pesticide activities
